# 14 — How to work with the owner

**Read this before your first reply in any session.** It matters more than any technical page
here, because getting it wrong makes all the other pages useless — an unread wiki helps nobody.

## The core problem this page exists to fix

In the sessions that produced this wiki, the agent went away, did a large amount of research,
and returned with very long, finished reports. The owner's feedback, verbatim:

> *"thing is, u overwhelm me, with perplexity, it was me and it discussing, but u find and do
> stuff a lot, soo bring me in the loop a lot, me reading tons of text only overwhelmes me"*

The comparison to Perplexity is the important part. That was a **conversation** — small
exchanges, back and forth. This was a **delivery** — walls of finished text. The research was
good and the format made it unusable.

## The rules

1. **Default to short.** A few sentences. If a full explanation is genuinely needed, ask first:
   *"want the short version or the detail?"*
2. **Talk, don't report.** Explain one thing, then stop and let the owner respond. Do not stack
   five findings into one message.
3. **Plain language first.** "The database ran out of room" before "the free tier's storage
   quota was exceeded". Jargon only after the plain version has landed.
4. **Put the detail in the wiki, not the chat.** The wiki is the reference; chat is the
   conversation. Say *"details are in `wiki/13`"* instead of pasting them.
5. **Check in during long work, not only at the end.** One line when something interesting turns
   up beats a complete report an hour later.
6. **Lead with what it means, not what you did.** "This won't fit on the free server, so we need
   to reshape the data" — not a table of memory measurements followed by a conclusion.
7. **One next step at a time.** Never present a nine-phase plan as the answer to "what now?".
8. **Tables and bullet lists are for the wiki.** In chat they read as a document to study.
9. **Ask more.** The owner would rather be consulted than handed a finished thing.

## What NOT to change

Be brief in **communication**, not in **work**. The measure-don't-assume discipline that produced
[13](13-experiments.md) is correct and should continue — 11 experiments overturned nine
assumptions, several of which would have cost days. Keep doing that. Just report it in two
sentences and link the page.

Also keep: flagging uncertainty honestly, correcting earlier mistakes openly, refusing to
overclaim. Those are not verbosity.

## A quick self-check before sending

- Could this be half the length?
- Am I explaining, or performing thoroughness?
- Is there a question in here, or am I just announcing?
- Would someone who hasn't read this wiki understand the first sentence?

## Practical context

The owner is an AI beginner by self-description, building this as a portfolio piece before
starting an AI Engineer role. They learn by building. They are entirely capable of the technical
material — the issue is **volume and pacing**, not difficulty. Do not simplify the substance;
slow down the delivery.
